## Design Context

### Users

Indonesian undergraduate students writing their **skripsi** (thesis) — and the lecturers / advisors who review those drafts. They span every discipline: engineering, biomedicine, law, humanities, education. They open CiteTrack at a desk, often during a high-stress revision pass days before a submission deadline. The job is concrete and self-defending: "find the language and citation problems my advisor will flag, before they do."

Many users are not technical and are not fluent in English. UI labels can be English, but user-facing prose, prompts, and explanations should sound like a thoughtful Indonesian editor — not a Silicon Valley product. The locale of the work product is Bahasa Indonesia; the locale of the *feelings* is academic Indonesia.

### Brand Personality

Three words, in priority order: **Trustworthy. Calm. Friendly.**

- **Trustworthy** comes first because students will defend the report to their advisor. The interface must read as carefully considered, never speculative or AI-handwavy. No confidence theater (no fake percentages, no "AI suggests…"). When the tool is uncertain, it says so plainly.
- **Calm** because thesis-writing is already stressful. Generous whitespace, no urgency theatre, no streaks/badges/celebrations.
- **Friendly** in the Indonesian sense: warm, helpful, addresses the user with respect ("Tahukah kamu?", not "Pro tip!"). Conversational without being playful.

The interface should feel like a thoughtful friend pulling out an annotated draft and pointing at the bits worth looking at — visually inviting, never grim, but never silly either.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Soft pastel + hand-drawn doodles + bold display sans.** Reference: the "Learny" educational-product look — soft yellow/mint/blush/sky sections, marker-style accent words, sparing illustrated doodles. Adopted in full visually; voice rules below temper it.

- **Layout is a stack of full-bleed colored bands.** Pages are composed by chaining `<Section tone="butter|mint|blush|sky|cream" />` blocks. Each section has a kicker, an accent-word display headline, body, and (where relevant) a doodle in the margin.
- **Display type is bold sans (Manrope ExtraBold).** Mixed-case headlines, tight tracking (`-0.022em`). Body is Inter at 400/500. Drop Fraunces / serif display entirely.
- **Accent words inside headlines.** Two treatments, used situationally per headline:
  - `<AccentInk>kata</AccentInk>` — colors one word coral or indigo
  - `<Marker tone="yellow|green|blush|sky">kata</Marker>` — soft pastel pill behind one word with a hand-drawn underline scribble
- **Color is soft, never bold.** Surfaces use `--bg-cream`, `--bg-butter` (yellow), `--bg-mint` (green), `--bg-blush` (red/pink), `--bg-sky` (blue). Only CTAs and accent words use the saturated coral / indigo / marker palette. Ink is near-black, not pure black; text-on-color is ink or white.
- **Doodles in `src/components/doodles/`** — Squiggle, DottedArc, Underline, Sparkles, Lightbulb, Arrow, StarBurst, PaperPlane. Stroke-only SVGs, used **sparingly** (one or two per band max). They're accent, not focal point.
- **Buttons are pills.** Filled coral primary, filled indigo secondary, cream-outline outline, ghost. Soft shadow on filled CTAs, tiny lift on hover.
- **Cards are rounded (1rem) and soft.** White or cream surfaces with `--line` borders. Pastel-fill cards use a darker pastel border that matches the tone.
- **Severity is pastel-coded.** Errors land in `--bg-blush` badges, warnings in `--bg-butter`, info in `--bg-sky`. Severity dots stay small.

**Theme**: Light-only. No dark mode.

#### Explicitly NOT

- **Mascot illustrations.** Doodle accents are in; characters with backpacks, faces, or limbs are out. The reference uses them; we don't, because the audience is thesis writers, not learners-of-courses.
- **AI / dashboard tropes**: purple→blue gradients, glowing dark UI, hero-metric layouts with sparklines, dense icon-prefixed identical-card grids that all look the same.
- **Confidence theatre**: streaks, badges, points, confetti, "great job!" toasts. Visual playfulness is in; performative encouragement is not.
- **Saturated everywhere.** The pastel surfaces are *soft*. The bright coral/indigo only appears in CTAs and one accent word per headline — never as a full section background.

### Design Principles

1. **Bands of color compose the page.** Stack `<Section tone="...">` blocks vertically. Each band carries one chunk of meaning. Never multiple bands of the same tone touching.
2. **One accent per headline.** Either `<AccentInk>` *or* `<Marker>`, on one word. Multiple coloured words in the same headline becomes a clown headline.
3. **Doodles are seasoning.** One squiggle in a corner, one arrow pointing at a callout — enough. If a page reads as "decorated", remove a doodle.
4. **The document is still the subject.** Even in the new aesthetic, on the review screens (Evaluation report, Track review phases, Results) the PDF preview and the findings table are the largest, most legible things on the page. Pastel surfaces frame them; doodles are not allowed inside the data area.
5. **Calm voice in warm seams.** Visual personality is loud now; written voice stays quiet. Use "kamu" not "anda"; "Tahukah kamu?" not "Pro tip!". No exclamations, emoji, or congratulatory micro-copy. The visuals carry the warmth; the words carry the credibility.
